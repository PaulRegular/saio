# NAFO beaked redfish in Division 3M, 2024 assessment

## Assessment and sources

The latest recoverable full age-based assessment is the 2024 XSA for the combined beaked redfish stock (*Sebastes mentella* and *S. fasciatus*) in Division 3M. The 2024 assessment was used for advice for 2025 onward. The 2026 assessment was deferred; the 2026 source review found no replacement production XSA or detailed fitted tables. The assessment applies to beaked redfish only; golden redfish (*S. norvegicus*) is excluded.

The separately listed NAFO SCR Doc. 24/032, “An update assessment of beaked redfish in Division 3M,” has no download link in the NAFO catalogue. The direct PDF URL `https://www.nafo.int/Portals/0/PDFs/sc/2024/scr24-032.pdf` returned not found when checked on 2026-11-04. The available Scientific Council report provides the model summary, assumptions and short-term projection table. Cached source files and checksums are listed in `provenance/nafo_redfish_3m_2024/manifest.csv`; cached PDFs remain local-only in the ignored `source_cache/` directory.

## Inputs and assumptions

The accessible sources do not provide the annual age-specific commercial catch, EU survey indices, weight-at-age or maturity-at-age matrices used in the 2024 XSA. The supporting report documents combined-species age-length keys and maturity information; 2020–2022 age-length information is combined across the two beaked redfish species and applied to 2023, for which age readings and maturity ogives were unavailable. Those descriptions do not permit the assessment input matrices to be reconstructed.

The assessment summary reports STACFIS estimated beaked redfish catch for 2015–2023 in thousands of tonnes. Those values are based on species proportions in observed catch, and are preserved as a separate total-catch series; they are not a substitute for the missing age-specific catches. Combined catch and TAC values for all three redfish species are not attributed to this beaked-redfish stock.

The 2024 XSA used ages 4–19+, the EU Flemish Cap summer survey, and the documented historical natural-mortality settings. From 2015 onward the assessment used M=0.10; earlier periods included higher M assumptions, including 0.4 tuned to ages 4–6 in 2006–2010 and applied to all ages in 2009–2010. Catchability was independent of stock size and age-independent from age 16; no tapered time weights were used. Terminal survivors were not shrunk to mean F, oldest-age survivors were not shrunk, and a minimum standard error of 0.5 was imposed at the last true age.

## Outputs and completeness

Table 7.1 in SCS Doc. 24/16 reports beginning-of-year 2024–2027 SSB, yield and TAC under seven scenarios. The canonical outputs table has no scenario field, so the numeric outputs retain the common deterministic 2024 values and the Fstatusquo scenario (F=0.0585) only. The other six scenarios remain in the cited source table; their labels and the selection are recorded in assumptions. The 2025–2027 SSB intervals are central 80% intervals, not 95% intervals.

Historical annual SSB, numbers-at-age, F-at-age, M-at-age, and age-specific XSA diagnostics are shown only in figures or are not available in the accessible material; they are not digitized. No accepted limit reference point exists. F0.1=0.0675 is a candidate reference point, while Fmax is considered too uncertain and is not accepted.

Assumptions, inputs and outputs are **partial**. The imported numerical data comprise the published beaked catch totals and the complete short-term projection table; important age-specific model inputs and fitted historical outputs remain unavailable. The 2024 SCR 24/032 assessment report is an explicit source-access blocker, not evidence that the underlying data do not exist.

This review records source facts and gaps only; it contains no downstream model settings or comparison results.
